Every laptop released in late 2026 has a new spec on the datasheet that didn't exist three years ago: TOPS.

Tera Operations Per Second. It's the measure of AI processing performance, and it's rapidly becoming more important than traditional specs like CPU clock speed or GPU cores.

The reason is simple: AI is moving from the cloud to your device. And running AI models locally requires specialized hardware that can deliver the computational performance without draining your battery in 90 minutes.

That hardware is the NPU (Neural Processing Unit), a dedicated AI accelerator built into modern processors alongside the CPU and GPU. It's not a minor addition. For many tasks, the NPU is now the most important processing unit in the system.

At VoltPak, we focus on portable power. We care about devices that work reliably when you're away from wall power. And NPUs are quietly revolutionizing what's possible on battery power. Here's what you need to know about the neural processing revolution and why it matters for portable computing.

## The Local AI Power Problem

Running AI models is computationally expensive. Large language models, image generation, real-time video processing, computational photography: these tasks require massive parallel processing that traditionally happened in data centers with megawatts of power available.

When you use ChatGPT or generate an image with DALL-E, you're sending a request to a massive server farm. The AI model runs on specialized GPUs or custom AI chips with hundreds of watts of power consumption. The result comes back to your device in seconds, but the computation happened elsewhere.

This cloud-based approach works well for some applications. But it has critical limitations:

**Latency:** Round-trip network delays add 50-200ms or more, making real-time applications sluggish.

**Privacy:** Every interaction with cloud AI means sending your data to external servers. For sensitive information, this is unacceptable.

**Connectivity:** No internet connection means no AI capabilities. Useless on planes, in rural areas, or anywhere with limited connectivity.

**Cost:** Cloud AI inference costs scale with usage. High-volume applications become prohibitively expensive.

**Reliability:** You're dependent on external services staying online and maintaining performance. Service outages mean your applications stop working.

The solution is obvious: run AI locally on the device. But the power requirements make this impractical on battery power with traditional architectures.

A high-end laptop GPU can deliver excellent AI performance, but it consumes 60-150 watts doing so. On a laptop with a 60Wh battery, that's 24-60 minutes of runtime running AI tasks continuously. Completely impractical for real-world mobile use.

The CPU is more power-efficient but far too slow for real-time AI inference. Running a modern language model on a CPU at usable speed would still drain a laptop battery in under 2 hours while making the device unusable for other tasks.

This is the local AI power problem: the computational requirements of AI tasks far exceed what traditional processors can deliver within portable power budgets.

Enter the NPU.

## What Makes NPUs Different

An NPU is a specialized processor designed specifically for neural network inference. Unlike CPUs (designed for general-purpose serial processing) or GPUs (designed for parallel graphics and compute tasks), NPUs are optimized exclusively for the matrix multiplication and tensor operations that dominate AI workloads.

This specialization enables dramatic efficiency improvements:

**Architectural optimization:** NPU silicon is designed around the specific operations neural networks use most frequently. No wasted transistors on instructions that AI models don't need. Every part of the chip serves AI inference.

**Memory architecture:** NPUs feature tightly integrated high-bandwidth memory positioned close to compute units, minimizing data movement. Memory bandwidth, not raw compute, often limits AI performance. NPUs solve this with dedicated memory hierarchies optimized for AI data access patterns.

**Low-precision arithmetic:** AI inference often works well with 8-bit or even 4-bit integers instead of 32-bit or 64-bit floating point. NPUs implement efficient low-precision units that deliver higher performance per watt. A CPU or GPU doing the same calculation in 32-bit floating point wastes energy on precision that doesn't improve results.

**Dedicated datapaths:** NPUs implement fixed-function units for common AI operations (convolution, pooling, activation functions, attention mechanisms). These units execute much faster and more efficiently than general-purpose programmable units doing the same work.

The result is measured in power efficiency: modern NPUs deliver 4-6x better performance per watt than GPUs for AI inference tasks, and 10-20x better than CPUs.

In practical terms, an NPU can run an AI workload that would consume 50 watts on a GPU while using just 8-10 watts. Same performance, dramatically less power consumption.

For portable devices, this is transformative. Suddenly continuous AI processing becomes feasible on battery power without destroying runtime or turning the device into a hot, noisy fan concert.

## The 40 TOPS Threshold

Microsoft established 40 TOPS as the minimum NPU performance for "Copilot+ PC" certification. This isn't arbitrary: 40 TOPS represents the threshold where local AI becomes genuinely useful for real-time applications.

At 40 TOPS, you can:

**Run large language models:** 7-13 billion parameter models execute with sub-second response times. Not GPT-4 scale (which requires far more compute), but models comparable to Llama 2 13B or Mistral 7B that deliver high-quality responses for most tasks.

**Process video in real-time:** Background blur, auto-framing, lighting correction, and noise reduction in video calls, all processed live without lag.

**Execute computational photography:** Multi-frame HDR fusion, night mode processing, portrait mode with realistic bokeh, all happening in the background as you shoot. No waiting for processing after the shot.

**Semantic search across local data:** Windows Recall-style features that index and search everything you've ever done on the device based on content meaning, not just file names.

**Real-time translation:** Live translation of video calls, instant translation of text on screen, continuous speech-to-text transcription with punctuation and formatting.

**Intelligent assistance:** Context-aware suggestions, autocomplete, summarization, and content generation that responds instantly because it's all local processing.

Below 40 TOPS, these applications either don't work at real-time speeds or work but drain the battery so quickly they're impractical for mobile use. Above 40 TOPS, they become seamless background capabilities.

Current generation NPUs from Intel (Arc), AMD (Ryzen AI), Qualcomm (Hexagon), and Apple (Neural Engine) all exceed 40 TOPS. High-end implementations reach 60-75 TOPS. This is the new baseline for premium portable devices.

## Power Efficiency in Practice

Let's look at real-world power consumption for a typical AI workload: running a 7B parameter language model for local chat assistance.

**CPU implementation:** 45-65 watts continuous power draw. Battery life: 1-1.5 hours on a 60Wh battery. System becomes hot and fans run at maximum speed. Unusable for real mobile work.

**GPU implementation:** 35-55 watts for a dedicated GPU, 15-25 watts for integrated graphics running the same model at similar speed. Battery life: 2-4 hours. Fans audible under load. Feasible but marginal for mobile use.

**NPU implementation:** 6-12 watts depending on model size and desired response speed. Battery life: 5-8 hours of continuous AI usage. Minimal heat generation. Silent operation. Completely practical for all-day mobile work.

The NPU doesn't just enable local AI. It enables always-on local AI that runs continuously in the background without destroying battery life or thermal performance.

This unlocks entirely new usage patterns:

**Proactive assistance:** The AI monitors what you're doing and offers suggestions without being asked because there's no power penalty for continuous operation. Writing an email? Suggested phrases appear based on context. Working in a spreadsheet? Anomalies are flagged automatically. Reading documentation? Key points are highlighted and summarized.

**Continuous processing:** Features like real-time transcription, automatic content categorization, and intelligent search indexing run all the time, building up contextual knowledge that makes the system more useful. With CPU or GPU power consumption, you'd enable these features only when specifically needed. With NPU power consumption, they're always active.

**Privacy-preserving intelligence:** All personal data stays local. The AI knows your work context, communication patterns, and workflow preferences because it processes everything locally. Nothing leaves the device. This level of personalized intelligence isn't possible with cloud AI where privacy concerns limit what data you're willing to share.

## Architecture and Integration

Modern NPUs aren't standalone chips. They're integrated directly into the main processor die alongside CPU cores and GPU units.

The Intel Core Ultra processors feature an NPU integrated into the SoC with dedicated memory bandwidth and power delivery. AMD Ryzen AI chips include a similar integrated NPU. Apple's M-series chips have integrated Neural Engines since the M1. Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite features one of the most powerful NPUs at 45 TOPS.

This integration is critical for efficiency. Data moving between separate chips consumes power and adds latency. An integrated NPU shares memory with the CPU and GPU, eliminating data transfer overhead. Power management is unified across the entire SoC, allowing sophisticated policies that route workloads to the most efficient processing unit.

The system automatically decides which processing unit handles each task:

- Simple AI inference with low latency requirements: NPU
- Complex AI training or very large models that don't fit in NPU memory: GPU
- Control logic and orchestration: CPU

Users never manage this manually. The OS and drivers handle workload routing transparently, optimizing for the best combination of performance, power consumption, and latency.

This heterogeneous computing architecture (multiple specialized processors working together) is the future of efficient computing. Single processors trying to do everything will lose to specialized units optimized for specific workloads.

## Real-World Applications

The NPU hardware exists. The power efficiency is proven. But what does it actually enable that wasn't possible before?

### Intelligent Video Conferencing

Modern video call quality depends heavily on AI processing: background blur or replacement, auto-framing that follows you around the room, lighting correction, noise suppression, echo cancellation.

Running this on a GPU works but consumes 15-25 watts continuously for the duration of the call. On a 3-hour video meeting, that's 45-75 watt-hours, enough to drain most laptop batteries completely even with minimal other usage.

Running the same processing on an NPU uses 3-6 watts. The battery impact becomes negligible. You can take long video calls on battery power without worrying about running out of charge.

The quality improvement is significant too. NPU-accelerated video processing enables more sophisticated algorithms that deliver better background separation, more natural lighting correction, and more effective noise suppression than GPU implementations could afford computationally.

### Local Language Models

This is the killer app for NPUs: running large language models entirely on-device for instant, private AI assistance.

Current 7-13 billion parameter models deliver quality comparable to GPT-3.5 for many tasks: summarization, rewriting, question answering, code generation, language translation. These models fit comfortably in 8-16GB of RAM and run at interactive speeds on a 40+ TOPS NPU.

The experience is transformative compared to cloud AI:

**Instant responses:** No network latency. Queries return in under a second.

**Unlimited usage:** No API rate limits or usage costs. Use it as much as you want without additional charges.

**Complete privacy:** All data stays on your device. Draft sensitive emails, analyze confidential documents, generate private content, all without sending anything to external servers.

**Offline capability:** Works on planes, in areas with no connectivity, anywhere. AI assistance is always available regardless of internet access.

**Personalized models:** The model can be fine-tuned on your specific data (writing style, domain knowledge, work context) without privacy concerns. This level of personalization isn't feasible with shared cloud models.

For professionals working with sensitive information (lawyers, doctors, financial advisors, journalists), local AI powered by NPUs enables capabilities that simply aren't possible with cloud services due to privacy and confidentiality requirements.

### Computational Photography

Smartphone cameras have used NPU-equivalent hardware (Apple Neural Engine, Google Tensor, Qualcomm Hexagon) for computational photography for several years. The techniques are now coming to laptop and tablet cameras, enabled by integrated NPUs.

Portrait mode with realistic depth of field. Multi-frame HDR that captures detail in both bright and dark areas simultaneously. Night mode that produces sharp, colorful images in near-darkness. All processed in real-time as you capture, with no post-processing delay.

This turns mediocre laptop webcams into surprisingly capable cameras that produce better images than dedicated cameras did a decade ago. The hardware remains simple and cheap. The NPU-accelerated processing makes up the difference.

## What This Means for Portable Computing

NPUs fundamentally change what's possible on battery power.

Devices that previously required wall power for AI-intensive work can now operate untethered all day. Workflows that were impractical due to battery drain become routine. Features that seemed futuristic become standard.

The implications for portable power demand are significant:

**Reduced peak power:** NPUs handle tasks that would otherwise spike GPU power consumption. This reduces both average and peak power draw, enabling smaller batteries or longer runtime from the same capacity.

**Improved efficiency:** More work gets done per watt-hour. Users accomplish the same tasks with less energy consumption. Battery life improves even as capability increases.

**Different charging patterns:** With longer battery life from NPU efficiency gains, users charge less frequently. This reduces wear on battery cells and extends overall battery lifespan.

For VoltPak's mission of reliable portable power, NPUs are a net positive. They make devices more energy-efficient, reducing the total power delivery required from portable power stations and power banks. They extend the usefulness of portable power by enabling longer untethered operation.

But they also raise the bar for what users expect from portable computing. AI assistance isn't a nice-to-have feature anymore. It's core functionality. And that functionality needs to work reliably on battery power, which means it needs efficient NPU acceleration.

## The Technical Tradeoffs

NPUs aren't perfect for all AI workloads. Several limitations constrain their applicability:

**Model size:** NPUs have limited onboard memory, typically 4-16GB for integrated implementations. Larger models that exceed this must run on GPU or CPU with external memory, losing the NPU efficiency advantage.

**Flexibility:** NPUs are optimized for inference (running existing models), not training (creating new models). Training still requires GPUs. The NPU handles deployment and usage of pre-trained models only.

**Software maturity:** Development tools and frameworks for NPU programming are less mature than GPU equivalents. Optimizing models specifically for NPU execution requires specialized knowledge and tooling that's still developing.

**Workload compatibility:** Some AI architectures map well to NPU hardware (transformers, convolutional networks, standard computer vision models). Others don't (sparse models, highly dynamic architectures, models with unusual operation sequences). These may perform better on more flexible GPU or CPU implementations.

The practical result is that NPUs handle the most common AI workloads very efficiently, while GPUs remain necessary for edge cases and specialized applications. Both have roles in a complete system.

## The Future: Scaling Up

Current NPUs in consumer devices deliver 40-75 TOPS. Next-generation implementations planned for 2027-2028 target 100-150 TOPS, approaching the capability of discrete GPUs while maintaining the power efficiency of integrated NPUs.

This additional performance enables larger models (20-30 billion parameters), more sophisticated multi-modal processing (simultaneous vision, language, and audio understanding), and real-time generative AI (generating images or video on-device without cloud processing).

The architecture is also becoming more flexible. Earlier NPUs were very rigid in what operations they supported efficiently. Newer designs add programmability while maintaining efficiency for common operations. This allows them to handle a wider range of AI models without falling back to GPU processing.

Memory bandwidth is improving too. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) integration is coming to laptop-class processors, delivering the memory bandwidth that very large models need to run at full speed. This removes one of the key constraints limiting NPU performance today.

Within 3-5 years, NPUs in portable devices will deliver performance comparable to current discrete GPUs for AI workloads, at a fraction of the power consumption. The distinction between "mobile" and "desktop" AI capability will largely disappear.

## The Bigger Shift

NPUs represent a broader trend in computing architecture: specialization.

For decades, general-purpose CPUs handled everything. Then GPUs emerged as specialized processors for graphics and parallel compute. Now NPUs join as specialized processors for AI, and other specialized units (media encoders, security processors, network accelerators) are integrating into SoCs alongside them.

The future of efficient computing is heterogeneous systems with multiple specialized processors, each optimized for specific workloads, working together under unified orchestration. The OS routes each task to the processor best suited for it, optimizing for the best combination of performance and power consumption.

This is already happening in smartphones (where SoCs integrate 6-8 different specialized processors) and it's rapidly coming to laptops and desktops. The PC of 2028 will have more in common architecturally with a smartphone than with a PC from 2020.

For portable computing, this shift is entirely positive. Specialized processors deliver far better performance per watt than general-purpose processors. Every workload that migrates from CPU/GPU to a specialized accelerator improves battery life and thermal performance.

NPUs are just the beginning. The next five years will see accelerators for media processing, encryption, networking, and other specialized tasks all integrating into portable device SoCs. Each one will reduce power consumption and extend battery life for its respective workload.

The end result is portable devices that are dramatically more capable than today's systems while consuming the same or less total power. More work done per watt-hour. Longer runtime from the same battery capacity. Portable power that enables more, not less.

And that's a future we're excited to power.
